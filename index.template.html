How to use this tool

What is a Bahtinov mask?

A Bahtinov mask is a cheap focusing aid for telescopes and camera lenses. It's a disk with a pattern of angled slits. Put it over the front of your scope, point at a bright star, and it turns the star into a spike pattern. You are in perfect focus when the middle spike sits exactly halfway between the two outer spikes. Off by a hair and the middle spike shifts — that's the whole trick.

Quick start

  1. Leave the defaults, but set A to your telescope's aperture (it auto-fills from M − 2R).
  2. If you know it, enter your F focal length — it tunes the slit size.
  3. Keep the slit pitch at Recommended.
  4. Download SVG to cut from paper/vinyl, or switch to 3D and Download STL to 3D-print.
  5. Point at a bright star, focus until the central spike is centered.

Parameter cheat sheet

MMask diameter — total width of the disk. Make it a bit larger than your scope's tube.
RRim width — the solid border that holds the slits together and rests on the tube.
AAperture — the clear opening. Auto = M − 2R, which is right for most scopes.
FFocal length — drives the recommended slit pitch. Longer focal length → finer slits.
SSlit pitch — spacing of the bars. Recommended is best; 3× gives brighter but coarser spikes.
TypeBahtinov = focus only. Tri-Bahtinov V2 = focus + check collimation (mirror alignment).
ObstructionTurn on Central obstruction only for reflectors with a secondary mirror; set it to the mirror's size.
RoundingHow rounded the slit corners are. Purely aesthetic — doesn't change the optics.

Tips

  • In 3D mode you can drag to rotate and scroll to zoom the model.
  • For a reflector, enable the central obstruction so light isn't blocked by the secondary.
  • The slit pitch only needs to be roughly right — focusing works across a wide range.
  • Use a bright star (or a distant point light) and high magnification for the clearest spikes.
